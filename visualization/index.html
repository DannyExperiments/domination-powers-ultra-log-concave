Dominating sets of powers of paths and cycles

In the ℓ-th power of a path or cycle, each vertex covers itself and every vertex within distance ℓ. The theorem says qk, the chance that a random k-set covers everything, is log-concave in k. Equivalently, R(k) = qk−1/qk, the chance that a dominating k-set survives deleting a random member, never decreases. Drag to rotate; click vertices to add or remove them.

Why it holds: write a dominating set as the gaps between chosen vertices, which are bounded by 2ℓ+1. Deleting a member merges two gaps. With one more gap in the rest of the circle, the merged pair is stochastically smaller, so it fits more often. Paths add an end-versus-interior comparison. Full proof in the paper.
